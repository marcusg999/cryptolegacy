I. the arrangement

Two channels.
Neither opens
anything alone.

channel one · held by us

The encrypted vault. Account access. Death verification. The audit log. All of it ciphertext, and none of it any use on its own.

channel two · held by you

The passphrase. On paper, in a safe deposit box, your attorney's safe, a steel plate. Wherever you decide — we are never told.

An executor with the passphrase and no account access holds ciphertext they cannot reach. A custodian with the sealed envelope cannot log in. Someone who compromises our entire infrastructure gets encrypted blobs.

III. redundancy or split

We show the arithmetic

Split the phrase across custodians and two different numbers matter. The one that governs is what a coalition of every holder but one would still have to guess — because that is a house fire plus one cooperative relative.

12 words ÷ 2 parts77.5 bits · acceptable
12 words ÷ 3 parts51.7 bits · marginal
12 words ÷ 4 parts38.8 bits · refused
15 words ÷ 3 parts64.6 bits · acceptable
24 words ÷ 3 parts103.4 bits · strong

Below forty bits the app refuses rather than warns. Splitting also means losing one part destroys access permanently — which makes it strictly more fragile than doing nothing.

IV. the refusals

What we will
not build

Not a will generator. We store what your attorney drafts. We never draft it.

Not a custodian. We cannot hold, move or spend your cryptocurrency.

Not a hint system. No riddles, no partial phrases. Cryptography, not obscurity.

Not able to read your vault. No server-side decryption path exists. Not for support, not for law enforcement, not for us.

Not a password reset. There cannot be one. Lose the paper and the vault is unreadable by anyone, permanently.
